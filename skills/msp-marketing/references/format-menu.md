# Format Menu: The Output Flavors

Every piece of content is one of these formats. Lengths are shipped example defaults (see
SKILL.md Setup Decisions) drawn from web-writing norms; tune them once real performance data
exists. All formats follow msp-brand voice and the SKILL.md style guardrails.

---

## 1. Full Resources Article (the anchor format)

**For:** the resources/blog section of {{DOMAIN}}. The canonical home of every topic.
**Length:** 600-900 words.
**Structure:**

- **Title:** benefit- or question-led, under 60 characters, no clickbait. Good: "How to Spot a
  Phishing Email Before You Click." Bad: "You Won't Believe What Hackers Are Doing Now."
- **Opening (2-3 sentences):** the reader's situation or problem, in their words. No throat-
  clearing, no "in today's world."
- **Body:** 3-5 sections with plain-language subheadings. Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences).
  Use a numbered list when giving steps, prose otherwise.
- **"What to do about it" section:** every article ends with concrete action the reader can take
  this week, even without hiring anyone.
- **CTA:** one, from the CTA library, as a natural closing paragraph.

**SEO checklist (required for this format only):**

1. One target phrase a business owner would actually type ("how to spot phishing email," not
   "email threat vectors"). Use it in the title, one subheading, and the first 100 words. Never
   stuff it.
2. Meta description: 140-155 characters, plain English, includes the phrase, promises the payoff.
3. 1-2 internal links to relevant pages on {{DOMAIN}} (services page, standing-offer page, or a
   prior resource) with natural anchor text.
4. Title options: deliver 2-3 title candidates with the draft.

## 2. Short Resource / Quick Tip

**For:** lighter website posts, professional-network articles, or a fast-turnaround piece.
**Length:** 150-250 words.
**Structure:** one problem, one fix, one CTA. A single tip done well, not a compressed article.

## 3. Professional Network Post

**For:** the company page and the owner's personal profile on your professional social network
(personal profiles typically outperform company pages; post to both, personal first).
**Length:** 100-200 words, first line under 80 characters (it's the hook before the fold).
**Structure:** hook line → the insight or micro-story in 2-4 short paragraphs → one question or
soft CTA. No hashtag walls; 0-3 relevant hashtags. No links in the body when avoidable (comment
or reshare with the link); if a link is needed, one only.
**Voice note:** slightly more personal than website copy. First person is welcome ("A client
asked me this week...", details anonymized per msp-sales case-study rules).

## 4. Local Business Listing Post

**For:** the company's local business listing profile, which feeds local search.
**Length:** 80-120 words visible; front-load the point in the first sentence.
**Structure:** local angle or timely hook → one useful point → CTA button text ("Learn more" /
"Call now") pointing at {{DOMAIN}}. Always attach an image (brand-styled per msp-brand; a brand
icon tile works when nothing better exists).

## 5. Newsletter Blurb

**For:** the monthly client/prospect newsletter (example default; see Setup Decisions).
**Length:** 50-100 words per item.
**Structure:** tease the article's payoff, link to the full resource. Written fresh, not the
article's first paragraph.

## 6. General Social Post

**For:** secondary, more casual social channels, when requested.
**Length:** 40-120 words. Warmer and more casual than the professional network, same guardrails.

## 7. Security Advisory (handoff format)

When the topic is an active threat and the audience is **existing clients**, this is not
marketing: load msp-client-comms and use its security advisory template. This skill may then
adapt the same topic into a public resources article (formats 1-4) with the client-specific
details removed. Public version educates; it never reveals anything about clients'
environments.

---

## Repurposing Chain Reference

Default chain per SKILL.md: Full article → professional network post → local listing post →
newsletter blurb. Each written fresh for its channel. Label each piece and separate with `---`
when delivering.
