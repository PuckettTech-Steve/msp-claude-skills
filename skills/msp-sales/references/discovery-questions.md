# Discovery Call Framework: 15 Questions

The goal of discovery is to understand their situation, uncover pain, and align on where they want
to go, so your proposal maps directly to their words, not your features.

**Rule of thumb:** Listen 70%, talk 30%. Your job in discovery is to ask good questions and
take good notes, not to pitch.

---

## How to Open Discovery

After your opener and permission:

> "Before I tell you anything about us, I want to make sure I understand your situation. Do you
> mind if I ask you a few questions about how things work today?"

Then: listen, take notes, ask follow-ups. Don't rush to fill silence.

---

## Section 1: Background (Understand Their World)

**B1. "How big is your team? How many people work here?"**
- Why: Helps scope the engagement; establishes whether they're in your ICP
- Listen for: Employee count, locations, remote workers, growth trajectory

**B2. "Who handles your IT today? Do you have someone in-house, or do you work with
an outside company?"**
- Why: Understand the current solution and what you're competing against
- Listen for: "We have a guy," "we use [MSP name]," "we kind of handle it ourselves"
- If they have a current MSP: "How long have you been with them? What's that relationship like?"

**B3. "What does your typical tech setup look like? Are you mostly PC, Mac, a mix?
Any servers on-site, or are you cloud-based?"**
- Why: Scope and complexity; identifies whether they have on-prem infrastructure
- Listen for: Old hardware, servers they "need to replace soon," hybrid environments

**B4. "What software does your business run on day-to-day? Anything industry-specific?"**
- Why: Uncovers line-of-business apps, licensing dependencies, integration complexity
- Listen for: their industry's line-of-business software (practice management, job costing,
  case management, and similar), plus general accounting software

**B5. "What do you use for email and collaboration today, one of the major cloud platforms,
or something else?"**
- Why: Immediately scopes a billable seat category; tells you about their cloud maturity
- Listen for: "We're still on an old on-premises mail server," "we're on a cloud platform but
  not sure which version"

---

## Section 2: Pain (Find What's Actually Broken or Feared)

**P1. "When was the last time IT caused a problem for the business, something that
slowed people down or cost you time or money?"**
- Why: Grounds the conversation in real experience, not hypothetical
- Listen for: Stories. Let them talk. Ask "what happened?" and "how long did it take to fix?"
- If they say "nothing lately": "Lucky. But is there anything that's been a slow burn?
  Things that work but you've been meaning to address?"

**P2. "What happens when someone has a computer problem or can't access something?
Who do they call, and how long does it usually take to get resolved?"**
- Why: Surfaces response time pain and support black holes
- Listen for: "They call me," "we wait days," "we just restart and hope," "nobody really knows"

**P3. "Have you had any security incidents? Viruses, phishing emails, anything like that?"**
- Why: Uncovers past trauma (huge buying trigger) and reveals current risk posture
- If yes: "Tell me about it. How did you handle it? What changed afterward?"
- If no: "Are you worried about it? Have you had any close calls?"

**P4. "How do you handle backups? Do you know where your data is backed up and
how quickly you could recover if something happened?"**
- Why: Most SMBs have inadequate or untested backups; this question usually surfaces anxiety
- Listen for: "I think we have something set up," "our server backs up overnight," "honestly I'm not sure"
- Follow-up: "If your server failed tomorrow morning, how long would it take to be back up and running?"

**P5. "Are there any compliance or insurance requirements around IT that you have to
meet, things like cyber insurance, HIPAA, PCI, or anything like that?"**
- Why: Compliance pressure is the #1 accelerator of MSP sales decisions
- Listen for: "Our insurance company is asking for documentation," "we're in healthcare so yes,"
  "we're not sure what we need to have"

---

## Section 3: Goals (Understand Where They're Headed)

**G1. "Where's the business going in the next 12-18 months? Are you growing, hiring,
opening new locations, anything like that?"**
- Why: Growth creates IT complexity and urgency; shows you think like a business partner
- Listen for: Hiring plans, office moves, new locations, acquisition activity

**G2. "What would 'great IT' look like for you? If everything was working the way you
wanted, what would be different?"**
- Why: Surfaces their vision so you can mirror it back in your proposal
- Listen for: "People wouldn't have to call me," "we'd stop losing time on IT problems,"
  "I'd actually know someone was watching things"

**G3. "Is IT something you think about a lot, or something you'd prefer to just not
have to think about?"**
- Why: Gauges how engaged they want to be vs. how much they want to hand off
- Most SMBs: "I'd love to not think about it" -> your value prop is peace of mind and removal
- Some: "I like to stay involved" -> your value prop is partnership and visibility

**G4. "What would need to be true for you to feel comfortable making a change here?
Is there anything specific you'd need to see or know?"**
- Why: Directly surfaces their buying criteria; shows respect for their process
- Listen for: References ("do you have other clients like us?"), pricing ("within budget"),
  trial/proof ("can we do a test?"), timeline ("we're not in a rush")

**G5. "Is there anything I haven't asked about that you think I should know about
your situation?"**
- Why: Open door for anything they've been holding; sometimes the most important thing comes here
- This question signals you're listening, not just running through a checklist

---

## The Bridge: After Discovery

Summarize what you heard before you say anything about your services:

> "Let me make sure I've got this right. You've got [X people], you're currently [current
> situation], and the things that are most on your mind are [pain point 1] and [pain point 2].
> You'd love to [goal]. Does that sound right?"

Wait for them to confirm (or correct you). Then:

> "That's really helpful context. Let me tell you a bit about how we work, and you can tell me
> if it sounds like a fit."

**Only now do you start talking about your services.**

---

## Key Follow-Up Questions to Have Ready

- "Tell me more about that."
- "How long has that been going on?"
- "What have you tried to fix it?"
- "What happened after that?"
- "How does that affect the team day-to-day?"
- "What would it cost you if that happened again?" (quantify pain)
