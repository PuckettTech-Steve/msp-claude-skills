# Email Templates & Sequence Frameworks

---

## Subject Line Formulas

Good MSP subject lines are short, specific, and curiosity-driven. Avoid buzzwords.

**Pain-based:**
- "Quick question about [Company]'s IT"
- "What happens when [Company]'s computers go down?"
- "IT question for [Company]"

**Social proof:**
- "[Local business type] we support. Worth a quick chat?"
- "How [similar company type] handles IT now"

**Direct:**
- "15 minutes: IT for [Company]?"
- "Managed IT for businesses like [Company]"

**Avoid:** "Exciting opportunity," "Revolutionary IT solution," anything with ALL CAPS or !!

---

## Email 1: Pain-Point Hook (First Touch)

**Length:** Under 150 words
**Goal:** Get a reply or a booked call, not tell them everything

---

**Subject:** Quick question about [Company]'s IT

Hi [First Name],

I work with small businesses in [area/industry] to take IT off their plate completely: one
flat monthly cost, everything handled.

Most of my clients came to me after something went sideways: a ransomware attack, an employee
leaving who was their "IT person," or just realizing their setup hadn't kept up with their growth.

I don't know if any of that resonates for [Company], but I'd love to find out.

Would you be open to a 15-minute call this week to see if it's worth exploring?

{{OWNER_NAME}}
{{COMPANY_NAME}} | {{PHONE}}

---

**Variation (cyber insurance angle):**

Subject: Cyber insurance question for [Company]

Hi [First Name],

I've been talking with a lot of businesses lately who are running into requirements from their
cyber insurance carriers: documentation, security controls, things like that.

If that's come up for [Company], I'd love to compare notes. We work with businesses your size
to handle exactly this kind of thing.

15 minutes this week?

{{OWNER_NAME}}

---

## Email 2: Social Proof (Day 5-7)

**Length:** Under 150 words
**Goal:** Build credibility with a "businesses like you" story

---

**Subject:** How a [similar business type] cut their IT stress in half

Hi [First Name],

Wanted to follow up on my note from last week.

We recently started working with a [similar business type] in [region]: [X] employees, no
dedicated IT person, kind of cobbled together over the years. Sound familiar?

Three months in, they haven't had a single "the whole office is down" situation. Their team
has one number to call. Their owner stopped getting calls on weekends.

Not sure if that's the kind of problem you're dealing with, but worth a quick conversation
to find out.

Still open to 15 minutes?

{{OWNER_NAME}}

---

## Email 3: Specific Value Prop (Day 10-12)

**Length:** Under 200 words
**Goal:** Connect a specific service to their likely situation

---

**Subject:** One thing most businesses your size don't have

Hi [First Name],

I've been thinking about [Company] since my last note.

One thing that comes up almost every time I talk with a business your size: they don't have a
real backup and recovery plan. Not "we back up to an external drive." I mean an actual plan
for what happens if the server fails, ransomware hits, or a key person leaves without warning.

We build that for every client we take on. They can tell me, within minutes, how long it would
take to recover from a worst-case scenario, because we've tested it.

I'd love to spend 15 minutes seeing if that's something worth solving for [Company]. No pressure,
no sales pitch, just a conversation.

{{OWNER_NAME}}

---

## Email 4: Breakup (Day 18-21)

**Length:** Under 100 words
**Goal:** Get a reply (even a "no thanks") or re-engage a cold lead

---

**Subject:** Closing the loop

Hi [First Name],

I've reached out a couple times and haven't heard back. Totally understand, busy season
happens.

I'll take this as my last note. If IT ever becomes a priority at [Company], I'd genuinely
love to talk. We're good at what we do and I think we'd be a fit.

Either way, best of luck.

{{OWNER_NAME}}

*P.S. If the timing's just bad, one-word reply works: "Later" and I'll follow up in a few months.*

## Sequence Spacing

| Email | When to Send | Notes |
|-------|-------------|-------|
| Email 1 | Day 1 | First touch, keep it short |
| Email 2 | Day 5-7 | Wait for a reply first; if nothing, send this |
| Email 3 | Day 10-12 | Value prop; specific to their situation if known |
| Email 4 | Day 18-21 | Breakup; get a yes or a no |

**After sequence ends:**
- Move to "Long-term nurture": reach out every 60-90 days with a relevant update, stat, or
  quick check-in. Many MSP deals close 6-18 months after first contact.

---

## Cold Email Best Practices

- **Personalize the first line** when possible: mention their industry, a recent hire, a news item
- **One ask per email**: don't offer a call, a demo, AND a resource in the same email
- **Plain text usually outperforms HTML** for cold outreach (feels more personal)
- **Send Tuesday-Thursday, 8-10am or 1-3pm** in recipient's time zone
- **Never attach anything** to a cold email (spam filters and distrust)
- **Follow up the same thread**: reply to your own email chain; don't start a new one
