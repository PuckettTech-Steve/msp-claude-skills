# Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

**Example default:** The size range and sweet spot below reflect one working MSP's target market
in one service area. Treat them as a labeled starting point, not a rule. Adjust the range,
industries, and geography to match the market you actually want to serve.

---

## Primary ICP: The Underserved SMB

**Size:** 10-100 employees (sweet spot: 25-75, example default)
**IT situation:** No dedicated in-house IT staff, currently reactive
**Current setup:** Break-fix/ad-hoc support, "a guy they call," or nothing
**Budget:** $1,000-$8,000/month in IT spending (often untracked)
**Pain level:** Medium to high: something has gone wrong recently or they fear it will

**Why they buy:**
- Cyber insurance renewal forced them to address gaps
- A recent incident scared them (ransomware, data loss, key person left who handled IT)
- Growth has outpaced their current setup ("we hired five people and things are falling apart")
- The "IT guy" they relied on retired, moved on, or started charging more
- They were referred by someone who trusts you

**Why they don't buy yet:**
- No immediate pain ("things are mostly fine")
- They assume IT support is too expensive for a business their size
- They don't realize what they're risking (data loss, compliance fines, productivity losses)
- They're loyal to someone they know, even if that person isn't serving them well

## ICP Scoring: Evaluate Prospects Here

Score each prospect before investing significant time:

| Signal | Weight | How to Find |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| 10-100 employees | High | professional social networks, website "About" page |
| No dedicated IT staff | High | Ask directly in discovery |
| Recent IT incident or scare | Very High | Ask about "last time something went wrong" |
| Compliance requirement (HIPAA, cyber ins., PCI) | Very High | Ask about insurance or industry reqs |
| Currently on break-fix or "have a guy" | High | Ask who handles IT now |
| Growing business (hiring, new office) | Medium | professional social networks, news, local business press |
| Decision-maker in conversation | High | Confirm who makes the call |
| Budget authority present | Medium | Ask early: "Who's involved in decisions like this?" |

**Green light:** 4+ high/very high signals -> pursue actively
**Yellow light:** 2-3 signals, missing decision-maker -> worth a follow-up, don't over-invest
**Red light:** Under 10 employees, no pain, no decision-maker -> low priority

## Top Verticals by Profitability

MSPs see higher margins and faster closes in regulated or risk-sensitive industries. The margin
figures below are industry-research data, not this business's own numbers:

| Vertical | Why They Buy | Avg. MSP Margin | Key Angle |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------|
| Manufacturing | Uptime, ransomware risk, OT/IT convergence | ~51% | "Downtime costs you production" |
| Healthcare / Medical | HIPAA, data security, uptime | ~34% | "Patient data is your liability" |
| Accounting / CPA | Client data, tax-season uptime, cyber ins. | ~33% | "Your clients trust you with their financials" |
| Legal | Confidentiality, ransomware target, bar rules | ~30% | "One breach ends your practice" |
| Finance / Wealth Mgmt | SEC/FINRA, cyber insurance mandates | ~30% | "You're a compliance-driven buyer" |
| Construction | Project management uptime, field + office | Medium | "Your team works everywhere, IT should too" |
| Professional Services | General SMB profile | Medium | Growth, reliability, cost predictability |

**Startup recommendation:** Don't niche yet. Build the pipeline broadly. But when you're cold
outreaching, customize your opening line to speak the vertical's language.

## Buyer Personas

### Persona 1: The Owner/Operator (Most Common)
- **Title:** Owner, President, CEO, Managing Partner
- **Size:** 10-50 employees
- **IT relationship:** Views IT as a cost center; has been burned before or is afraid of being burned
- **Primary fear:** Data loss, ransomware, "we get hacked and go out of business"
- **What they want:** Simple, predictable, someone they can trust
- **How to sell:** Build personal trust first. They don't buy services, they buy *you*.
- **What to avoid:** Jargon, lengthy technical explanations, feature lists
- **Magic words:** "predictable monthly cost," "one call and it's handled," "we're your IT team"

### Persona 2: The Office Manager / Operations Lead
- **Title:** Office Manager, Operations Manager, COO, Practice Manager
- **Size:** 30-100 employees
- **IT relationship:** Handles IT problems by default because no one else does; frustrated
- **Primary fear:** Being blamed when something breaks; staff complaining about tech
- **What they want:** Someone to take IT off their plate completely
- **How to sell:** Empathize with the burden. "You shouldn't have to deal with this."
- **What to avoid:** Making them feel responsible for the decision (they often need owner buy-in)
- **Magic words:** "we handle everything," "you'll never have to restart the router again," "one number to call"

### Persona 3: The Compliance-Driven Buyer
- **Title:** CFO, HR Director, Practice Manager, Risk Manager
- **Industry:** Healthcare, legal, finance, accounting
- **IT relationship:** Was just told by insurer/auditor/attorney that their IT situation is a liability
- **Primary fear:** Regulatory fines, losing insurance coverage, legal exposure
- **What they want:** Documented compliance, a partner who understands their requirements
- **How to sell:** Lead with compliance outcomes. Show you understand their specific requirements.
- **What to avoid:** Generic pitches. They need to know you understand HIPAA/PCI/cyber insurance
- **Magic words:** "cyber insurance requirements," "documented controls," "compliance-ready"

### Persona 4: The Tech-Aware Skeptic
- **Title:** Any title, but has some technical background
- **Size:** Any
- **IT relationship:** Has managed IT themselves or previously worked with an MSP that failed them
- **Primary fear:** Overpaying for something they can manage themselves; being oversold
- **What they want:** Proof that you're technically competent and transparent
- **How to sell:** Be direct. Show your process. Let them ask hard questions.
- **What to avoid:** Overselling, vague promises, buzzwords
- **Magic words:** "here's exactly how we handle that," "let me show you our process"

## Buying Signals: When to Push

These signals indicate a prospect is closer to buying than their words suggest:

- Asks about pricing unprompted ("so what does something like this typically cost?")
- Mentions a specific recent incident ("we had a ransomware thing last year")
- References a compliance deadline ("our cyber insurance renewal is in March")
- Asks "how long does onboarding take?"
- Starts using "when" instead of "if" ("when we switch over...")
- Refers to your competitor negatively without prompting
- Asks who your other clients are (reference check = they're serious)

**When you see 2+ buying signals:** Ask for the next step directly.
"It sounds like the timing might be right. Should we schedule a time to go through a proposal?"
